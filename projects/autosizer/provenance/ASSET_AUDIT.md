## Method and final-paper comparison

The minimum scientific unit is a circuit netlist/specification plus two coupled loops. The LLM analyzes circuit roles and trade-offs, prioritizes variables and chooses their discrete ranges/fixed values. A numerical inner loop proposes sizes using a selected algorithm, obtains actual electrical measurements from ngspice, and accumulates history. The controller chooses subsequent algorithms/batch sizes. Stagnation, infeasibility and boundary concentration feed an outer revision of ranges or active variables. Feasibility is a conjunction of metric constraints; the paper's objective is a product of target-normalized maximize metrics divided by normalized minimize metrics. A high objective alone does not establish feasibility.

The final PDF was downloaded (712,545 bytes, SHA-256 `0a4326aad8d813a6337115ecf1980d6829fd87f69d439e4133edc47548e91e0e`) and inspected as text. arXiv v2 is dated May 28, 2026 and was read through public web HTML/PDF extraction; its direct cloud PDF download was denied. Compared sections show the same two-loop mechanism, 24 SKY130 tasks, inference settings, candidate allocation and three-trial protocol. Table 2 values inspected agree, including the AutoSizer FOM/evaluation/runtime/SR rows. This is a targeted substantive comparison, not a byte-level or exhaustive editorial diff. No asset-relevant version change was identified in those sections.

Both describe Gemini-2.5-Flash, temperature 0.4, top-p 0.85, top-k 20 and 8,192 maximum output tokens. They allocate up to three outer cycles with 100 candidate samples each, for 300 total, and compare GA, BO, TuRBO plus three standardized LLM baseline reimplementations. Main evaluation uses circuit simulation; layout and post-PEX support are optional extensions in repository documentation. Neither source establishes a training prerequisite or a local GPU requirement.

## Verified static discrepancies and testable gaps

1. **Controller setting drift — confirmed source/paper discrepancy.** `llm_guided_ota_optimization_test.py:260` sets maximum output tokens to 65,536, versus 8,192 in both paper versions. Temperature/top-p/top-k match. This changes the potential inference budget; actual token consumption and performance effects are unknown.

2. **Budget semantics — documentation error confirmed; fair-budget behavior needs testing.** README describes `max_total_designs=100` as shared across cycles. In source a new optimizer is created each cycle (`:2241` vicinity), its history starts empty, and `:2272`–`:2278` applies the limit within that cycle; histories combine at `:2575` after cycles finish. The code therefore resembles the paper's three-times-100 allocation rather than README's global 100. The caller passes a whole requested batch without a visible remaining-budget clamp (`:2375` vicinity). `all_searched_designs` is appended only when a result is returned (`iterative_ota_optimization_test.py:2674` vicinity). Future accounting must distinguish proposals, unique designs, failed simulations, successful results and actual ngspice subprocess calls, especially for multipoint characterization. No overshoot was experimentally observed.

3. **Measurement concern — explicit questionable scaling confirmed; physical impact unverified.** Folded-cascode YAML sets `dc_gain_db > 1.5`, `power_dc < 1.0` while marking processed power as µW, and multiplies the SPICE supply-power expression by 100 before a further 1e6 post-scale (`folded_cascode_ota.yaml:200`). [Issue #4](https://github.com/yuxi120407/AutoSizer/issues/4) independently questions these values and reports runtime observations; those observations are third-party evidence, not this audit's results. Do not conclude that a YAML's dimensional FoM fallback is necessarily used: the controller's `_get_fom_metric()` first regenerates a normalized expression and overwrites `metric_post.fom.expr` on success (`:430`–`:466`). That limits one criticism in the issue, while leaving raw-measurement scaling and feasibility targets to resolve.

4. **Reported hard-task failures — partly corroborated, not blanket verified.** [Issue #3](https://github.com/yuxi120407/AutoSizer/issues/3) reports LDO, bandgap and AZC failures. Current bandgap YAML/helper still show four-node PNP instances and an extra external PNP model path; exact compatibility requires the intended SKY130 model definition. Current LDO/AZC width lists still include large values; validity depends on PDK bins and device multiplicity conventions. Crucially, the current LDO wrapper casts `ibias` to float (`iterative_ota_optimization_test.py:1571`), so the issue's older YAML string arithmetic failure is not established on that route. Static YAML parsing does reveal many exponent-form values as strings, but that is not itself proof of a simulation bug.

5. **Invalid-result handling — confirmed unsafe branch; triggering frequency unknown.** `search_designs()` constructs `results_sorted=[]` when returned results exist but every FoM is invalid, then indexes `[0]` (`iterative_ota_optimization_test.py:2709`). This can mask the measurement failure. No triggering execution was attempted.

6. **History and baseline fidelity — scientific gaps to test.** Inner numerical history is reset by a fresh optimizer at each outer cycle, with structured feedback driving regeneration and all histories combined afterward. Quantify the benefit of reflective decisions versus ordinary restarts with equal simulation cost. Standalone authors' LEDRO/ADO-LLM/EE-Sizer standardized reimplementations and TuRBO runner were not found in the inspected root/source inventory. They may exist elsewhere; full comparison completeness remains unconfirmed. Inner pool methods alone do not fill this gap.

7. **Scope of feasibility.** A single nominal process-corner specification does not establish process/voltage/temperature robustness, mismatch yield or post-layout stability. Test these as extensions after learning the core mechanism; their absence from a chosen pilot is not a verified error in the reported nominal task. Cross-circuit normalized FoM averaging also merits sensitivity analysis because task scales and feasible regions differ.

## Proposed future pilot and substitutions

Nothing in this section was executed. Start with `telescopic_ota.yaml` (four width variables and supplied gain/bandwidth/power measurements), keeping the public topology, testbench and SKY130 device family. Pin the audited commit and a documented PDK/ngspice revision; verify metric units and all constraints before optimization. Supply missing Python packages, PDK paths and a user-authorized hosted model connection. Use the paper's 8,192-token limit. Optional layout dependencies can be deferred without losing the main electrical-sizing mechanism.

Minimum faithful implementation retains circuit understanding, discrete variable selection, at least two numerical optimizer choices, actual SPICE feedback, and at least one opportunity for outer revision. A canned JSON controller replay only checks plumbing; an analytic objective or mock simulator cannot establish analog-sizing behavior.

**One useful mechanism test:** compare the full outer reflection loop against a condition freezing its initial active variables/ranges, while retaining the same inner algorithm pool/controller protocol, task and total attempted-candidate budget. Three seeds per arm; three cycles capped at 30 proposals each gives at most 540 proposals across six trials. This reduced budget is a mechanism pilot, not the paper's 300-per-trial evaluation. Record actual simulator invocations separately, best feasible FoM versus attempts, first-feasible evaluation, boundary concentration, variable reactivation, invalid measurements, wall time and API tokens. Log whether revised ranges escape the initial domain and whether that yields feasible designs. A null result or lack of stagnation on one easy circuit does not falsify the framework; it may require a harder prevalidated task.

## Concrete costs and confidence

Cheap route planning estimate, not measured requirement: 2–4 CPU cores, 8–16 GiB RAM and a few GiB of disk headroom for a minimal model setup, Python environment and bounded outputs; current cloud CPU/RAM look adequate. The author paper does not report hardware/RAM requirements, and exact SKY130 installation size was not downloaded/measured. No GPU, training dataset or checkpoint download is required with hosted inference. One candidate may invoke several SPICE analyses; do not equate 540 proposals to 540 processes. Allow an explicit pilot API cap (for example at most 20 calls per trial, with an independently approved token/spend cap); this is a proposed cap, not a measured call count or dollar estimate. Endpoint quota, price and historical model availability remain unknown.

Estimated integration effort for a single validated task: roughly 0.5–2 engineer-days, contingent on simulator/PDK access and metric clarification. This is an audit judgment, not an author claim. Full comparison nominal ceiling: 24 tasks × 3 trials × 300 = 21,600 candidate proposals for AutoSizer alone; seven methods would reach 151,200 before early stopping, ablations or reruns. Actual electrical runs can exceed proposals. Reconstructing comparison runners and adjudicating per-task definitions could take several engineer-days to weeks; no evidence supports a specific GPU-hour or API-dollar estimate. Full layout adds compiler/solver/layout dependencies, the missing PEX wrapper, PDK integration and circuit coverage work; do not assume 30 GiB disk is sufficient for that stack.

Go for a future bounded pre-layout pilot after dependency/PDK preparation, measurement review and authorized API access. No-Go for claiming direct reproduction, complete 24-task success, paper baseline fairness or layout closure from this audit. Code and text-asset existence confidence is high; executable completeness and resource estimates are medium; paper-level electrical correctness remains unverified.
