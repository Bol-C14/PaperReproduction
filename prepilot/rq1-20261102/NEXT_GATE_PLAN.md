## Engineering threshold scale — a screening hypothesis, not significance

The 50 ps threshold is a **prospectively specified engineering screening hypothesis** for the next selected protocol. It is not a community standard, an empirically calibrated minimum detectable effect, a confidence bound, a physics/tool uncertainty bound, or a completed preregistration. Its purpose is to avoid investing in a new action/model study for tiny same-state differences before demonstrating a meaningful decision problem.

Scale anchors for this checkpoint: 50 ps is about 3.2% of the frozen 1.570 ns contest clock period, and about 2.0% of the 2.455 ns delay proxy `period − reported no-edit WNS`. Under the contest's algebraic proxy it corresponds to approximately 2.1% relative frequency improvement near this baseline, **not measured hardware Fmax**. It is 50 of the 1 ps displayed report increments and 5× the separately proposed 10 ps within-arm range screen. Those multiples are conservative operating choices; report precision is not measurement accuracy, and the 10 ps range screen is also a hypothesis, not measured tool noise. The 23 ps exploratory difference is below this practical screen, so repeating it is not the current priority.

If future requirements make a smaller difference valuable, revise the engineering threshold in a newly dated protocol **before** its new data, with its cost/utility rationale. Do not retrospectively tune this threshold or switch metrics to promote an observed winner. The 50 ps scale is tied to this checkpoint's current units; it is not automatically a cross-design absolute threshold.
